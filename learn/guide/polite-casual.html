Guidebook

ていねい・ふつう · Polite & Casual

Most of what you learn first is polite style. This page lines it up with plain style, which you need for friends and for many grammar patterns.

When to use which

Taught in Lesson 20. When in doubt, stay polite.

Who or whereStyleWhy
People you have just metPoliteYou don't know them well yet.
Teachers, bosses, older peoplePoliteIt shows respect.
Shops, offices, phone callsPoliteIt's the normal public style.
Letters, emails and speechesPoliteIt's the safe, standard style.
Close friends and familyPlainPolite style would sound distant.
Children and people younger than youPlainIt sounds natural and warm.
Diaries, notes, newspapers, essaysPlainThere is no listener to be polite to.
Plain style to a teacher or boss sounds rude. Mixing the two with the same person sounds odd.

Nouns

Nouns work exactly like な-adjectives.

TensePolitePlainMeaning

Negativeあめじゃ ありませんあめじゃ ないit isn't rainy

Never put だ after an い-adjective: おいしいだ is wrong.

How casual speech sounds

Friends drop some words as well as changing the forms.

What changesPoliteCasualMeaning
Questions drop ka and rise in toneコーヒーを のみますか。コーヒー、のむ？Will you have a coffee?
Yes becomes a short soundはい、のみます。うん、のむ。Yes, I will.
No becomes a short soundいいえ、のみません。ううん、のまない。No, I won't.
Nouns and na-adjectives drop da in questionsあした ひまですか。あした ひま？Are you free tomorrow?
Some particles drop outごはんを たべましたか。ごはん、たべた？Have you eaten?
Asking for an explanation with a soft endingどこへ いきますか。どこへ いくの？Where are you going?
A shorter word for 'but'ちょっと たかいですが、かいます。ちょっと たかいけど、かう。It's a bit expensive, but I'll buy it.
ぼく and きみ belong to casual speech too. ぼく is used by men and boys.

Plain forms inside polite sentences

Many patterns need a plain form in the middle even in a fully polite sentence. Only the very end of the sentence shows politeness.

PatternExampleMeaning
～と おもいますあした あめが ふると おもいます。I think it will rain tomorrow.
～まえにねる まえに、ほんを よみます。I read a book before I go to bed.
～ことが できますかんじを よむ ことが できます。I can read kanji.
～ときひまな とき、さんぽします。When I'm free, I go for a walk.
