Guidebook

しけん · JLPT N5 Exam

How the N5 test is set up and scored, what each question type asks you to do, and how to prepare. The facts here come from jlpt.jp, where you should check the current rules before you sign up.

What N5 tests

N5 checks basic Japanese. You should be able to read common phrases and sentences in kana and simple kanji, and pick out key details from short, slow conversations about everyday life and the classroom.

The three test sections

The test is 90 minutes of sitting time in three parts. Listening can run slightly differently depending on the length of the recording.

SectionOfficial name (kanji)TimeWhat it covers
Language Knowledge: Vocabulary言語知識（文字・語彙）20 minutesReading kanji, spelling, and word meaning.
Language Knowledge: Grammar, and Reading言語知識（文法）・読解40 minutesGrammar in sentences and short texts, then reading passages.
Listening聴解30 minutesRecorded conversations and short exchanges.
All answers are multiple choice, on a computer-marked sheet. Most questions have four choices, and some Listening questions have three.

Scoring and pass marks

Three sections are sat, but only two scores are reported: vocabulary, grammar and reading are combined into one.

Scoring sectionOfficial name (kanji)Score rangeMinimum to pass
Language Knowledge and Reading言語知識（文字・語彙・文法）・読解0 to 12038
Listening聴解0 to 6019
Total総合得点0 to 18080
You must clear all three lines. A total of 100 is still a fail if Listening is 18.

How the rules work

RuleWhat it means for you
Scores are scaledThe score comes from a statistical method, not from adding points per question, so you can't turn a number of right answers into an exact score.
You must sit every sectionMissing any section means you fail, and no scores are given for the sections you did take.
No partial retakesEach attempt is judged as a whole. You can't keep a good section from an earlier sitting.
Two sittings a yearThe test is held in July and December. Some cities overseas offer only one of them.
No official word listThe organisers don't publish lists of vocabulary, kanji or grammar. Every N5 list, including the one in this game, is a best estimate.

Vocabulary section: question types

PartOfficial name (kanji)What you do
1. Kanji reading漢字読みA word in a sentence is in kanji. Pick how it's read in hiragana.
2. Orthography表記A word is in hiragana. Pick how it's written in kanji or katakana.
3. Contextually-defined expressions文脈規定A sentence has a gap. Pick the word that fits the meaning.
4. Paraphrases言い換え類義Pick the sentence that means about the same as the one given.
Wrong choices in part 1 often differ by one small detail, such as a dakuten, a small っ or a long vowel.

Grammar section: question types

PartOfficial name (kanji)What you do
1. Sentential grammar 1文の文法1（文法形式の判断）A sentence has a gap. Pick the particle or form that fits.
2. Sentential grammar 2文の文法2（文の組み立て）Four pieces are jumbled. Put them in order and mark the one that lands on the star.
3. Text grammar文章の文法A short text has gaps. Pick what fits the flow of the text.
For the star question, build the full sentence first, then read off the piece on the star.

Reading section: question types

PartOfficial name (kanji)What you do
4. Comprehension (short passages)内容理解（短文）Read a text of about 80 characters on study, daily life or work, then answer.
5. Comprehension (mid-size passages)内容理解（中文）Read a text of about 250 characters on everyday topics, then answer.
6. Information retrieval情報検索Find what you need in something like a notice or timetable, about 250 characters long.
The numbering carries on from the grammar part, so reading starts at part 4.

Listening section: question types

PartOfficial name (kanji)What you do
1. Task-based comprehension課題理解Hear the question, then a conversation. Pick what the person should do next.
2. Comprehension of key pointsポイント理解Hear the question first, then listen for that one detail.
3. Verbal expressions発話表現Look at a picture with an arrow on one person. Pick what that person should say.
4. Quick response即時応答Hear a short remark, then pick the best reply. Nothing is printed.
In parts 1 and 2 the question comes before the conversation, so you know what to listen for. Part 4 has no pictures, so you have to catch the words.

Study tips

AreaWhat to do
ListeningPractise it every day. It has its own pass mark, and a strong reading score can't rescue it.
Listening on the dayEach recording plays once, so if you miss one, mark your best guess and move on to the next.
Numbers and timeDrill dates, times and prices out loud. Listening questions often turn on them.
VocabularyLearn the kanji reading and the kana spelling together, down to the long vowels and small letters.
ParticlesLearn them in whole sentences. Grammar part 1 often asks you to choose one.
Grammar and ReadingThis section's 40 minutes must cover both. Don't let grammar eat your reading time.
Information retrievalRead the question first, then search the notice for just that detail.
Practice testsDo at least one full run in real time. The N5 mock exam in this game is a good start.
Answer sheetAnswer every question. A blank can't earn anything.
