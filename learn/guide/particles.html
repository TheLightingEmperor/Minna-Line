じょし · Particles

Particles are the small words that show what job each word does in a sentence. Use this page when you can't remember which one goes where.

は (wa): the topic

は sets up what you are talking about, and the rest of the sentence comments on it. As a particle it is read wa, not ha.

UseExampleMeaning
Topic of the sentenceわたしは がくせいです。I am a student.
A time or place as the topicきょうは やすみです。Today is a day off.
A short question with the rest left outおなまえは？May I have your name?
Contrast between two thingsにくは たべますが、さかなは たべません。I eat meat, but I don't eat fish.
Making the object the topic (it replaces the object particle)ひるごはんは しょくどうで たべます。As for lunch, I eat it in the cafeteria.
Added after another particleにほんには やまが たくさん あります。In Japan there are a lot of mountains.
は replaces を and が. It never sits next to them (no をは). It stacks after に, で, へ, と and から: には, では, へは.

が (ga): the subject, and 'but'

が points at the thing itself, often as new information. It also marks the object of words such as すき, わかります and ほしい.

UseExampleMeaning
What existsつくえの うえに ほんが あります。There is a book on the desk.
What you haveきょうは じかんが ありません。I don't have time today.
What you like, dislike, or are good or bad atわたしは ねこが すきです。I like cats.
What you understandすこし にほんごが わかります。I understand a little Japanese.
What you wantあたらしい パソコンが ほしいです。I want a new computer.
What you can doわたしは にほんごが できます。I can speak Japanese.
A question word as the subjectだれが きましたか。Who came?
The answer to that questionやまださんが きました。Mr. Yamada came.
Reporting something you see or noticeあめが ふって います。It's raining.
A feature of the topicぞうは はなが ながいです。Elephants have long trunks.
The subject inside a describing clauseこれは わたしが きのう かった ほんです。This is the book I bought yesterday.
'But', joining two sentencesにほんの たべものは おいしいですが、たかいです。Japanese food is delicious, but expensive.
A soft opener before a requestすみませんが、しおを とって ください。Excuse me, but please pass the salt.
A question word is never followed by は. Say だれが and なにが, never だれは.

を (o): the object, and the path

を marks what the action is done to. With movement verbs it marks the place you leave or pass through. Read it o.

UseExampleMeaning
Direct objectパンを たべます。I eat bread.
Object of a do-verbテニスを します。I play tennis.
Place you leaveへやを でます。I leave the room.
Vehicle you get offでんしゃを おります。I get off the train.
Place you crossはしを わたります。I cross the bridge.
Corner you turn atかどを まがります。I turn at the corner.
Area you walk throughこうえんを さんぽします。I take a walk in the park.
Getting on is に (でんしゃに のります). Getting off is を (でんしゃを おります). Same for entering (に はいります) and leaving (を でます).

に (ni): a point in time, a place, a person

に pins something to one exact point: a time, a spot, a person or a result. It has more N5 uses than any other particle.

UseExampleMeaning
Exact clock timeろくじに おきます。I get up at six.
Date or monthじゅうがつ とおかに にほんへ きました。I came to Japan on October 10th.
Destinationらいねん にほんに いきます。I will go to Japan next year.
Arrival pointえきに つきました。I arrived at the station.
Where something or someone isこうえんに いぬが います。There is a dog in the park.
Where you live or stayおおさかに すんで います。I live in Osaka.
What you get on, enter or sit onいすに すわります。I sit on the chair.
Where you put or write somethingここに なまえを かいて ください。Please write your name here.
The person who receivesともだちに プレゼントを あげます。I give a present to a friend.
The person you get something fromちちに とけいを もらいました。I got a watch from my father.
The person you meet, ask or phoneせんせいに ききます。I ask the teacher.
Purpose of goingスーパーへ かいものに いきます。I go to the supermarket to shop.
How many times per periodいっしゅうかんに にかい テニスを します。I play tennis twice a week.
The result of a changeいしゃに なりたいです。I want to become a doctor.
What you chooseコーヒーに します。I'll have coffee.
Good or bad forたばこは からだに わるいです。Cigarettes are bad for your health.
Time words such as きょう, あした and まいにち take no に. See the Time page.

で (de): where and how

で marks where an action happens and what you use to do it.

UseExampleMeaning
Place of an actionとしょかんで べんきょうします。I study at the library.
Place of an eventホテルで パーティーが あります。There is a party at the hotel.
Way of travellingバスで いきます。I go by bus.
Toolはしで たべます。I eat with chopsticks.
Languageにほんごで はなします。I speak in Japanese.
Materialかみで はこを つくりました。I made a box out of paper.
Reason (after a noun)かぜで がっこうを やすみました。I missed school because of a cold.
How many people do itみんなで うたいましょう。Let's all sing together.
Aloneひとりで いきます。I'm going by myself.
Totalぜんぶで せんえんです。It's 1,000 yen in total.
The group you pick the best fromくだものの なかで りんごが いちばん すきです。Of all fruit, I like apples best.
On foot has no で: あるいて いきます.

へ (e): direction

へ shows the direction of movement. As a particle it is read e, not he.

UseExampleMeaning
Where you goらいげつ きょうとへ いきます。I'm going to Kyoto next month.
Where you come toともだちが うちへ きます。A friend is coming to my house.
Where you return toくにへ かえります。I'm going back to my country.
The way you turnみぎへ まがって ください。Please turn right.

と (to): and, with, quoting

と links nouns, marks who you do something with, and closes a quote.

UseExampleMeaning
And (a complete list of nouns)パンと たまごを たべます。I eat bread and eggs.
Together with a personともだちと えいがを みます。I watch a film with a friend.
The partner in an actionやまださんと けっこんします。I'm marrying Mr. Yamada.
Naming the two things you compareコーヒーと こうちゃと どちらが すきですか。Which do you like more, coffee or tea?
Quoting words「ありがとう」と いいました。I said 'thank you'.
Reporting a thoughtあした あめが ふると おもいます。I think it will rain tomorrow.
An automatic result (after a dictionary form)この ボタンを おすと、ドアが あきます。If you press this button, the door opens.
As 'and', と joins nouns only. To join verbs use the て form, and to join adjectives use くて or で.

も (mo): also, even

も adds 'too'. It takes the place of は, が and を, and it sits after other particles.

UseExampleMeaning
Also (in place of the topic)わたしも がくせいです。I am a student too.
Also (in place of the object)パンも たべます。I eat bread too.
Added after another particleきょうとへも いきました。I went to Kyoto too.
Both A and Bにくも さかなも たべます。I eat both meat and fish.
Neither A nor Bにくも さかなも たべません。I eat neither meat nor fish.
Question word with a negativeきのう なにも たべませんでした。I didn't eat anything yesterday.
Not even onceいちども いった ことが ありません。I have never been, not even once.
Stressing a large numberなんかいも いきました。I went many times.

の (no): linking nouns

の joins two nouns, and the first one describes the second. Think of it as a 's that works for everything.

UseExampleMeaning
Ownerわたしの かばんです。It's my bag.
Where you belongだいがくの せんせいです。I'm a university teacher.
What it is aboutにほんごの ほんです。It's a Japanese language book.
Where it comes fromイタリアの くつです。They are Italian shoes.
Positionつくえの うえに あります。It's on the desk.
Timeあしたの ごご あいましょう。Let's meet tomorrow afternoon.
Two names for the same personともだちの やまださんです。This is my friend Mr. Yamada.
Standing in for a noun ('one')この かさは わたしのです。This umbrella is mine.
A noun before 'before'しょくじの まえに、てを あらいます。I wash my hands before meals.
A noun before 'when'こどもの とき、よく あそびました。When I was a child, I played a lot.
Casual question endingどこへ いくの？Where are you going?
の comes after nouns only. い-adjectives go straight onto the noun (たかい やま) and な-adjectives use な (しずかな まち).

か (ka): questions and 'or'

か at the end of a sentence turns it into a question. Between nouns it means 'or'.

UseExampleMeaning
Yes or no questionこれは ほんですか。Is this a book?
Question with a question wordこれは なんですか。What is this?
Choosing between twoコーヒーですか、こうちゃですか。Is it coffee or tea?
'Or' between nounsコーヒーか こうちゃを のみます。I'll drink coffee or tea.
Invitingいっしょに いきませんか。Won't you come with me?
Offering helpてつだいましょうか。Shall I help?
Taking in news (falling tone)そうですか。I see.
With a question word, some unknown thingなにか たべましたか。Did you eat anything?
Polite questions end in か and a full stop in writing. The ？ mark is for casual sentences that drop か.

や (ya): and so on

や lists a few examples and hints that there are more. Add など to make that clear.

UseExampleMeaning
A few examples from a longer listかばんの なかに さいふや かぎが あります。There's a wallet, keys and so on in my bag.
With an 'and so on' word at the endスーパーで パンや たまごなどを かいました。I bought bread, eggs and so on at the supermarket.
と gives the whole list. や gives a sample of it.

から (kara): from, because

から marks a starting point. After a whole sentence it means 'because'.

UseExampleMeaning
Starting timeくじから はたらきます。I work from nine.
Starting placeうちから えきまで あるきます。I walk from home to the station.
Where you come fromインドから きました。I came from India.
The person something comes fromともだちから てがみが きました。A letter came from a friend.
Because (the reason comes first)あついですから、まどを あけます。It's hot, so I'll open the window.
Because (giving the reason alone)じかんが ありませんから。Because I don't have time.
After doing (after a te form)ごはんを たべてから、でかけます。I'll go out after I eat.
The reason goes in front of から, so the word order is the reverse of English 'because': reason, から, result.

まで (made): until, as far as

まで marks the end point of a time or distance. It often pairs with から.

UseExampleMeaning
Until a timeごご ごじまで はたらきます。I work until 5 p.m.
As far as a placeえきまで あるきます。I walk as far as the station.
From one point to anotherげつようびから きんようびまで はたらきます。I work from Monday to Friday.
By a deadlineごじまでに かえります。I'll be back by five.
まで means something goes on up to that point. までに means one action happens some time before that point.

より (yori): than

より goes after the thing you are comparing against. The adjective itself does not change.

UseExampleMeaning
A is more than Bこの かばんは あの かばんより おもいです。This bag is heavier than that bag.
With the preferred side statedりんごより みかんの ほうが すきです。I like mandarins more than apples.
より sits on the side that loses the comparison. ほうが sits on the side that wins.

ね (ne): right?

ね at the end of a sentence asks the listener to share your feeling or confirm something.

UseExampleMeaning
Agreeing about something you both noticeいい てんきですね。Nice weather, isn't it?
Showing sympathyたいへんですね。That must be hard.
Checking a factあしたは やすみですね。Tomorrow's a day off, right?
Thinking for a momentそうですね。Let me see.

よ (yo): you know

よ at the end of a sentence tells the listener something they may not know.

UseExampleMeaning
Telling someone something newこの ケーキは おいしいですよ。This cake is delicious, you know.
Reassuringだいじょうぶですよ。It's fine, really.
Gently correctingちがいますよ。No, that's wrong.
Warningもう じゅうじですよ。It's already ten o'clock, you know.
ね means 'we both think this'. よ means 'you may not know this'. Too many よ can sound pushy with a teacher or boss.

Classic confusion: は or が

With は the listener already knows the thing and the news comes after it. With が the thing itself is the news.

SituationWith はWith がWhat changes
Saying who you are, or picking yourself outわたしは やまだです。わたしが やまだです。First: I'm Yamada. Second: I'm the one called Yamada.
A known thing, or a newly mentioned thingほんは つくえの うえに あります。つくえの うえに ほんが あります。First: the book is on the desk. Second: there is a book on the desk.
Asking about a topic, or asking who did itあの ひとは だれですか。だれが きましたか。First: who is that person? Second: who came?
Contrast, or a plain statementテニスは すきです。テニスが すきです。First: tennis, I do like (maybe not other things). Second: I like tennis.
Quick test: if the question word comes last (あの ひとは だれですか), は marks the topic before it. If the question word is the subject (だれが きましたか), it takes が.

Classic confusion: に or で for a place

に goes with being somewhere, arriving or putting. で goes with doing something there.

With にMeaningWith でMeaning
こうえんに いぬが います。There is a dog in the park.こうえんで あそびます。I play in the park.
おおさかに すんで います。I live in Osaka.おおさかで はたらいて います。I work in Osaka.
つくえに ほんを おきます。I put a book on the desk.つくえで ほんを よみます。I read a book at the desk.
ホテルに とまります。I stay at a hotel.ホテルで しょくじします。I have a meal at a hotel.
An event is the exception. It happens rather than just sits, so it takes で even with あります: ホテルで パーティーが あります.

Classic confusion: に or へ

With verbs of going, coming and returning, both are correct and mean almost the same. Everywhere else に has jobs that へ can't do.

What you want to sayParticleExampleMeaning
Going somewhere, focus on the destinationににほんに いきます。I'm going to Japan.
Going somewhere, focus on the directionへにほんへ いきます。I'm going to Japan.
Arrivingにえきに つきました。I arrived at the station.
Enteringにへやに はいります。I go into the room.
Timeにくじに いきます。I'll go at nine.
Giving to a personにともだちに あげます。I give it to a friend.
へ only ever shows the direction of movement. If nothing is travelling (a time, a receiver, where a thing is), use に.

Classic confusion: を or が with すき, わかります, あります

These words describe a feeling, ability or state, not an action done to something, so the thing takes が. Action verbs take を.

WordParticleRightWrongMeaning
すきですがねこが すきです。ねこを すきです。I like cats.
きらいですがさかなが きらいです。さかなを きらいです。I dislike fish.
じょうずですがえが じょうずです。えを じょうずです。I'm good at drawing.
わかりますがにほんごが わかります。にほんごを わかります。I understand Japanese.
ありますがじかんが あります。じかんを あります。I have time.
ほしいですがくるまが ほしいです。くるまを ほしいです。I want a car.
できますがにほんごが できます。にほんごを できます。I can speak Japanese.
たべますをりんごを たべます。りんごが たべます。I eat an apple. (The wrong one says the apple is eating.)
かいますをパンを かいます。パンが かいます。I buy bread.
～たいです is the one that can take either: おすしを たべたいです and おすしが たべたいです are both fine.
