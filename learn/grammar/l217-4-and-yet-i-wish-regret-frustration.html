～のに (sentence ending)

And yet! I wish ~ (regret, frustration)

How to build it
V plain + のに。～ばよかったのに。～たらよかったのに。
What it means

Ending a sentence with のに shows regret or frustration that things did not go as hoped.

  • The second half is left unsaid.
  • Expect ばよかった and たらよかった before it.
  • Often aimed at the listener, so use it with care.
  • せっかく often adds to the feeling.
Your tone carries this ending: it falls and trails away with a small sigh.
Common set phrases
JapaneseMeaning
言えばよかったのにyou should have said
行けばよかったのにyou should have gone
せっかく来たのにand I came all this way
Example sentences
  1. 手伝てつだってくれたらよかったのに。Tetsudatte kuretara yokatta noni.I wish you had helped me.
  2. せっかく作つくったのに。Sekkaku tsukutta noni.And I went to all that trouble to make it.
  3. 試験しけんに合格ごうかくすると思おもっていたのに。Shiken ni gōkaku suru to omotte ita noni.And I was so sure I would pass the exam.
Common mistakes
  • 言ってくれるよかったのに。 → 言ってくれればよかったのに。
    Use the ば-form or the たら-form before よかった.
