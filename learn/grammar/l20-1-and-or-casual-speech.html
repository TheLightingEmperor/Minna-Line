Polite style vs plain style

です and ます, or casual speech

How to build it
Polite: です, ますPlain: だ, dictionary form, ない-form, た-form
What it means

Polite style (です/ます) is for people you don't know well, superiors, and formal situations. Plain style (dictionary form, ない, た, だ…) is for close friends, family, and people younger than you — and it's also the style of diaries, notes and newspapers. Gotcha: mixing the two in one conversation with the same person sounds odd; switching to plain with a superior sounds rude.

  • Plain style is the casual way of speaking.
  • Don't mix the two styles with the same person.
  • Plain style with a superior sounds rude.
