Make, let and cause a feeling

Different meanings of the causative

How to build it
V causative + て くれますN を + feeling verb causative
What it means

The causative can mean make, let or cause a feeling, and it only works when the person acted on is not above you.

  • Context tells you whether it's make or let.
  • ～させて くれます clearly means someone let you.
  • Feeling verbs take を: びっくりさせます, しんぱいさせます.
  • Never use the causative on someone above you.
Sorry to keep you waiting is おまたせしました, and it's built on this same causative.
Three meanings
MeaningJapaneseEnglish
Makeやさいを たべさせますmake someone eat vegetables
Letすきな ものを たべさせますlet someone eat what they like
Cause a feelingともだちを びっくりさせますsurprise a friend
Cause a feelingおやを あんしんさせますput your parents at ease
Keep waitingともだちを またせますkeep a friend waiting
Example sentences
  1. 父ちちは 私わたしを 外国がいこくへ 行いかせて くれました。Chichi wa watashi o gaikoku e ikasete kuremashita.My father let me go abroad.
  2. 母ははは 子こどもに すきな 服ふくを 着きさせて います。Haha wa kodomo ni suki na fuku o kisasete imasu.The mother lets her child wear any clothes they like.
  3. モチの ニュースは 町まちの 人ひとを びっくりさせました。Mochi no nyūsu wa machi no hito o bikkuri sasemashita.The news about Mochi surprised the people in town.
Common mistakes
  • せんせいに かんじを かかせました。 → せんせいに かんじを かいて いただきました。
    You can't make someone above you do things.
