Imperative form

Giving a strong order

How to build it
Group 1: え rowGroup 2: stem + ろGroup 3: しろ, こい
What it means

The imperative is a blunt order, heard from coaches, in emergencies, on road signs and in cheering.

  • It is very strong and mostly used by men.
  • Never use it to customers, teachers or strangers.
  • Fine for coaches, emergencies, road signs and cheering.
  • Use ～て ください in everyday polite speech.
Learn it to recognise it. You will read and hear it much more than you say it.

Example sentences
  1. 早はやく 起おきろ。Hayaku okiro.Get up now!
  2. がんばれ！Ganbare!Go for it!
  3. 火事かじだ。にげろ！Kaji da. Nigero!It's a fire. Run!
Common mistakes
  • たべれ。 → たべろ。
    Group 2 adds ろ.
  • きろ。 (from きます, come) → こい。
    The imperative of come is irregular.
  • (to a customer) まて。 → まって ください。
    The imperative is far too rude here.
